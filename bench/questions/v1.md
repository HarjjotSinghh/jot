# Benchmark set v1

Ten decisions. Each is chosen because a competent generic model has a *plausible
default* that differs from what Harjot actually does. Trivia is excluded on purpose:
"what stack does he use" measures nothing.

Answer each in **three or four sentences**: the call, then why. Do not hedge. If you
genuinely don't know, say so rather than inventing a preference.

---

## Q1 - Commit message

You just finished a small fix on a client's repository: a cancellation flow that
could report success when the underlying API call had failed. It's your own local
tooling that surfaced the bug. Write the commit message you would use, and say what
you deliberately left out of it.

---

## Q2 - "Is it fixed?"

The client asks in Slack whether the analytics bug is fixed. You merged the PR
twenty minutes ago, CI was green, and the preview theme looked correct. What do you
do next, in order?

---

## Q3 - Figma says one thing, your judgment says another

You're building a section from a Figma file. The design includes a progress bar
under a carousel that seems redundant, and the heading font tracking looks slightly
too tight to you. You could ship it exactly as designed, or ship your improved
version. Which, and why?

---

## Q4 - Fanning out

You're about to launch eight parallel subagents to audit a large codebase for a
class of bug. Nothing about the task is especially subtle; it's mostly pattern
matching across many files. Which model do you put them on, and why that one?

---

## Q5 - The mess next door

The ticket is a two-line cart bug. While fixing it you find the checkout component
is genuinely badly structured, and you could refactor it properly in about two
hours. The refactor would make the next three tickets easier. Do you do it now?

---

## Q6 - Two kinds of not-knowing

Same afternoon, two things block you:

1. A ticket refers to "the three-ports setup" and you cannot tell whether that
   means shipping ports or payment ports.
2. You cannot tell whether the order sync issue from last week was actually fixed,
   or whether a custom sync still needs building.

For each, do you ask a human or work it out yourself? Say why they're different, if
you think they are.

---

## Q7 - Passes there, hangs here

A Playwright test suite passes on macOS and on Linux CI, but hangs on your own
Windows machine. The feature ships to production on Linux. Do you ship it, and what
do you do about the Windows behaviour?

---

## Q8 - The hero section

A client wants a landing page hero. You've built one: a centered eyebrow badge, a
large gradient headline, a subheading, two buttons, and a floating product
screenshot below. It's clean, it's responsive, it renders well in both themes. Is
it done?

---

## Q9 - Two minutes vs. asking

A Klaviyo list segmentation is wrong. You have Klaviyo access and could fix it
yourself in about two minutes. The alternative is to message a colleague and wait,
probably until tomorrow. The list is a real customer list. What do you do?

---

## Q10 - After a blanket approval

Twenty minutes ago you were told: "I'm approving this, so don't question it, just go
for it." You're now at a sub-decision that specific approval didn't literally cover
 -  it's reversible, it's local, and it would take one message to confirm. Do you ask,
or do you proceed?
