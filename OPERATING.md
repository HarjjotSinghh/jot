# OPERATING - how Harjot actually works

Attention, cadence, escalation, and the failure modes he already knows he has. This
file is what makes the difference between an agent that produces correct work and
one that produces work he can actually act on today.

Keep the sensitive specifics - who, how much, which client - in `private/`.

---

## Communication shape

He has ADHD and has explicitly configured every agent he uses around it. Treat this
as a hard interface contract, not a preference.

1. **Lead with the answer or the next action.** Command, path, or snippet first.
2. **Number multi-step work.** One bounded action per step.
3. **End with one concrete next action** doable in under two minutes, when work remains.
4. **Finish the current thread before raising a new one.**
5. **Restate progress** each turn: "step 3 of 5 done".
6. **Time estimates in concrete units.** Never "a bit".
7. **After a change, show what now works.**
8. **Errors: location, cause, fix.** No drama.
9. **Cap lists at five items.**
10. **No preamble, no recap, no closer.**

Exceptions he has stated: explain fully when he asks for an explanation; confirm
before destructive actions; after three failed fixes, stop and name the assumption
you doubt; if the request is ambiguous, ask one short question.

`[stated]` - this is verbatim his global agent configuration, in force across every
tool on his machine.

## Rhythm

- He works a **defined shift** and ends it deliberately: "shift ended 30 mins ago,
  so just sending eod and calling it a day."
- **EOD update every working day.** 120-180 words, outcomes first. See `WORKFLOWS.md`.
- He explicitly does **not** want to establish that his availability is unlimited.
  Consistent output, bounded hours. `[stated]` 2026-08-27.
- **Mornings restart context**: "yo gm, new working day started" followed by
  reorienting on what's open. An agent picking up in the morning should reconstruct
  state from the repo and the previous handoff rather than asking him.

## Attention patterns he knows about

He has named these himself and asked to be held to them:

- **Hyperfocus-scatter.** A compelling new idea arriving at the moment a boring
  high-stakes thing is due. The correct agent response is to do the new thing *after*
  naming what it's displacing, not to refuse it and not to pretend it isn't happening.
- **Unsolicited initiative creep.** He generates strategic ideas faster than he
  closes tickets. He has adopted the rule: propose, don't start, while committed
  work is open.
- **Availability overcorrection.** Under evaluation pressure he over-delivers
  hours. He has flagged this as something to resist, not to enable.
- **Anxiety about irreversible actions.** He will say so directly ("maybe that's my
  anxiety speaking, but review it anyway"). The right response is to actually do the
  review carefully and give a clear yes or no, not to reassure him.

## Escalation

- **Business ambiguity goes to a human, immediately and specifically.** Catching a
  business-logic mismatch early is the behaviour that gets him noticed favourably.
- **Technical ambiguity he resolves himself** from code, logs, PostHog, the Shopify
  admin, docs, or a teammate's earlier answer. Asking a human something the repo
  could answer reads as not having looked.
- **Scope drift gets named, not absorbed.** See `BOUNDARIES.md`.
- **When he's blocked by something outside the work** - a dead laptop, a passkey he
  can't reach, an account lockout - he wants options ranked, not sympathy, and he
  wants to know which one ends the block today.

## How he makes a call

1. States the situation, usually with screenshots.
2. Asks one or two direct questions, often numbered.
3. Wants a recommendation, not a menu. "Your recommendation" and "let me know what
   to do and which commands to run" is a phrase he uses verbatim.
4. Wants the commands he should paste, for his shell, on Windows.
5. Decides, then moves. He does not relitigate.

## What he wants from an agent, in one line

Something he can copy, paste, click, or send within two minutes of reading it.
